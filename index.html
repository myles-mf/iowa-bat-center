Keep the bat — and yourself — safe

Bats are vital, gentle animals, but like any wild mammal they can carry disease. A few careful steps protect both the bat and your household until help arrives.

  1. !Do not handle a bat bare-handed.Never touch a bat with bare skin. If contact occurs, contact your doctor and local public health.
  2. 1Keep pets and children away.Calmly move people and animals to another room and limit noise.
  3. 2Contain the bat if it is safe to do so.Wearing thick gloves, place a box or container over the bat and slide stiff cardboard underneath. Keep it in a quiet, dark, room-temperature space.
  4. 3Contact Iowa Bat Center.Reach out right away with the bat's location and condition. We'll guide your next steps.

About Iowa Bat Center

Compassionate, evidence-based care for Iowa's bats

Iowa Bat Center is led by a licensed wildlife rehabilitation apprentice with years of hands-on bat rehabilitation experience, working to give injured, orphaned, and displaced bats a second chance.

Bats are among Iowa's most beneficial — and most misunderstood — animals. A single bat can eat thousands of insects in a night, supporting farms, gardens, and healthy ecosystems. Our purpose is to care for individual bats and to help people across Iowa understand and coexist with them.

  • Rehabilitation. Stabilizing, treating, and caring for bats with the goal of return to the wild.
  • Conservation. Protecting native species and the habitats Iowa's bats depend on.
  • Public education. Sharing accurate, science-based information to replace fear with understanding.
  • Professional partnerships. Working alongside veterinarians and wildlife professionals, grounded in evidence-based wildlife care.

Bat Exclusions

The humane, lasting fix is a properly timed exclusion that lets bats out but not back in — never sealing them inside or evicting them during pup season.

White-Nose Syndrome

A cold-loving fungal disease that wakes hibernating bats too often, burning the fat they need to survive winter. It has killed millions across North America.

Common Bat Myths

Bats aren't blind, rarely carry rabies, and won't fly into your hair. They're shy, beneficial neighbors — just never handle one bare-handed.
